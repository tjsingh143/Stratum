# Scenario S3: a correct split-plot (SYNTHETIC)

Generated by `scenarios/run.py S3` from `writeup.md.in`; every number below is filled from
`output/results.json` or `output/comparison.json`. The data are synthetic and deliberately
constructed (DECISIONS.md D-086): they show how Stratum behaves, not how a real process
varies.

## What the experiment appears to ask

Do the four etch settings differ, do the four positions on a wafer differ, and does the
position pattern depend on the etch setting? Each setting was randomized to three wafers,
and every wafer was read once at each position: 48 readings.

## What the design actually supports

All three questions, each against its own error term. Etch setting was applied to whole
wafers, so it can only be judged against how much wafers given the same setting differ
from each other: there are 8 degrees of freedom for that.
Position and the interaction vary within each wafer, so every wafer gives its own
within-wafer comparison, and they are judged against the within-wafer error on
24 degrees of freedom.

## The key result, correct and naive side by side

| Question | Correct analysis | Naive single-error analysis |
|---|---|---|
| Etch setting | F = 1.8 on (3, 8), p = 0.225 | F = 6.745 on (3, 32), p = 0.00118 |
| Position | F = 8 on (3, 24), p = 0.000722 | F = 0.6745 on (3, 32), p = 0.574 |
| Interaction | F = 0.3333 on (9, 24), p = 0.955 | F = 0.0281 on (9, 32), p = 1 |

The naive analysis pools both kinds of variation into one error term on
32 degrees of freedom. That makes two mistakes at
once. It treats the four readings on a wafer as if they were four independently treated
wafers, so it judges etch setting against within-wafer noise that is far smaller than the
wafer-to-wafer variation the setting has to beat. Here that turns a non-significant
result into an apparently significant one. And it mixes the large wafer-to-wafer variation
into the error for position, which hides a position effect the correct analysis detects.

## Why the error term matters here

The variance components make the gap visible: wafer-to-wafer variation is
0.9775, within-wafer error is
0.09. A difference between etch settings
has to be judged against the larger one. The allocation analysis states the same thing as
a planning fact: the standard error of an etch-setting difference is
0.8165 now, and no number of extra
readings per wafer can bring it below
0.8073;
Robust conclusion: only additional wafers lower the floor.

## What to conclude

With these data, the etch settings are not shown to differ: the correct test, on
8 degrees of freedom, does not reject. The positions do
differ, and the position pattern is not shown to depend on the setting. The naive
analysis would have reported the opposite on both main effects. Position was not
randomized (it is a property of the location), so its difference describes the wafers
rather than proving a cause.

Preregistered expectation: 58 of 58 assertions
match (status MATCH).
